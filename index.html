A sensor never reports the present. It reports the past, smoothed. This toolkit lets you see how far behind a sensor is, and learn to measure it yourself.

Try it: the tea test

A thermometer with a time constant of 10 s sits in room air at 20 °C. You drop it into tea at 80 °C. Move the slider to choose when you read it.

63.2% at τ = 10 s95% at 3τ
20 °C · air80 °C · tea

The thermometer is perfectly calibrated. It is only late. That lateness is what the four modules below explain.

Four symbols to know

You will meet these in every module. Here is what each one does, in plain words.

K
Static gain. Where the output ends up for a constant input. It sets the size of the response, not its speed.
τ
Time constant, in seconds. How slow a first-order sensor is. After τ it has done 63.2% of the change, after 3τ it has done 95%.
ζ
Damping ratio. How much a second-order sensor rings. Below 1 it overshoots, at 1 it is fastest without overshoot, above 1 it is sluggish.
ωn
Natural frequency, in rad/s. How fast a second-order sensor moves. Double it and every time on the curve halves.
