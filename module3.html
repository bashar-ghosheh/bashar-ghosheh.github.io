Second-order response

Tareq Ahmad · Dr. Wasel Ghanem

Apply a constant force of 1 N to a mass on a spring. Its displacement may overshoot equilibrium, while friction removes energy until the mass comes to rest.

What to try
  1. Compare all three damping presets.
  2. Increase mass and watch the poles move.
  3. Follow the phase path to its resting point.

Displacement under a unit force

Rise spans 10–90% of equilibrium. Settling uses the last crossing of the ±2% band.

State space and poles

Displacement is the first state and velocity is the second. Force drives acceleration through 1/m.

Phase plane

Follow the trajectory from rest to equilibrium. Crossing zero velocity marks a turning point.

Sensor glossary
τ
Time constant in seconds; a step reaches 63.2% of its final change at τ.
ω and ωₙ
Input angular frequency and undamped natural frequency, in rad/s.
M and φ
Output/input amplitude ratio and output phase relative to the input.
ζ
Damping ratio. Values below one allow oscillation.
Overshoot and settling
Excess above the final value, and the time until the response stays within its specified band.
Poles and eigenvalues
The roots of the transfer denominator and the characteristic roots of the state matrix. Both describe the natural motion here.
