Guess the sensor

Ahmad AlSheikh · Dr. Wasel Ghanem

A sensor with unknown parameters is on your test bench. All you get is one noisy response curve. Read the curve and work backwards, the way an engineer does with a real sensor.

How to play
  1. Look. Decide if the sensor is first order or second order.
  2. Measure. Use the level ruler and the time inspector to read values off the dots.
  3. Estimate. Type your numbers and lock them in. The page shows the true sensor on top of your guess.

Every estimate within 10% of the true value earns full marks. Need help? Ask for a hint: hints are free.

      Sensor glossary
      τ
      Time constant in seconds; a step reaches 63.2% of its final change at τ.
      ω and ωₙ
      Input angular frequency and undamped natural frequency, in rad/s.
      M and φ
      Output/input amplitude ratio and output phase relative to the input.
      ζ
      Damping ratio. Values below one allow oscillation.
      Overshoot and settling
      Excess above the final value, and the time until the response stays within its specified band.
      Poles and eigenvalues
      The roots of the transfer denominator and the characteristic roots of the state matrix. Both describe the natural motion here.
