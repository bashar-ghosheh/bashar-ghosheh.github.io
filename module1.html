First-order time response

Bashar Ghosheh · Dr. Wasel Ghanem

A sensor never reports the present instantly. In a mercury thermometer, the thermal capacitance C and thermal resistance R smooth and delay the reading. See how the static gain K and the time constant τ = RC shape its response to step and ramp inputs.

What to try
  1. Keep the step input and double τ. Watch the 63.2% marker move.
  2. Change K. Notice that the size changes but the timing does not.
  3. Switch to the ramp and find the lag between input and output.

Sensor glossary
τ
Time constant in seconds; a step reaches 63.2% of its final change at τ.
ω and ωₙ
Input angular frequency and undamped natural frequency, in rad/s.
M and φ
Output/input amplitude ratio and output phase relative to the input.
ζ
Damping ratio. Values below one allow oscillation.
Overshoot and settling
Excess above the final value, and the time until the response stays within its specified band.
Poles and eigenvalues
The roots of the transfer denominator and the characteristic roots of the state matrix. Both describe the natural motion here.
